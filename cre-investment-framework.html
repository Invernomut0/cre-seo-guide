📋 The 5-Step CRE Analysis Framework

Step 1: Property Data Collection

    ✓What to gather: Property type, square footage, lot size, year built, zoning, current use, occupancy status ✓Key documents: Current rent roll, operating expenses, property tax records, Zillow/Redfin comparables ✓Pro tip: Property type dictates your entire analysis. Don't analyze a retail property like multi-family.

Step 2: Income Analysis

    ✓Gross Scheduled Income (GSI): Total potential rent if fully occupied at market rates ✓Effective Gross Income (EGI): GSI minus vacancy/credit losses (typically 5-10% vacancy) ✓Revenue drivers: Rent escalations, CAM recoveries, parking income, amenities ✓Red flag: If 1+ tenant = 40%+ of income, that's concentration risk.

Step 3: Expense Analysis

    ✓Operating expenses: Property taxes, insurance, maintenance, utilities, management fees ✓Net Operating Income (NOI): EGI minus operating expenses (before financing) ✓Expense ratios by property type: Office (30-45%), Retail (15-25%), Multi-family (25-35%), Industrial (10-20%) ✓Watch for: Below-market rents creating artificially high NOI (rent roll vs. market rent gap)

Step 4: Valuation & Returns

    ✓Cap Rate: NOI ÷ Purchase Price (use our free Cap Rate Calculator) ✓Cash-on-Cash Return: Annual pre-tax cash flow ÷ Total cash invested ✓Equity multiple: Total cash distributed ÷ Total equity invested (over hold period) ✓Compare: Property cap rate vs. market cap rate (buy below-market cap, sell at market cap)

Step 5: Risk Assessment & Exit Strategy

    ✓Risk factors: Tenant credit, lease expiry schedule, property condition, market absorption, interest rate exposure ✓Value-add opportunities: Rent increases, expense reductions, repositioning, renovation, change of use ✓Exit strategies: Hold for cash flow, value-add flip, 1031 exchange, sell to institutional buyer ✓Always ask: "What am I buying that others aren't?" (Information asymmetry = your edge)

💡 Quick Tips for CRE Investors

    📌Buy based on NOI, not rent: Below-market rents = upside. Above-market rents = risk. 📌Cap rates vary by property TYPE and market: Compare apples to apples. Don't compare retail cap to office cap. 📌1% rule is a starting point, not gospel: Monthly rent ≥ 1% of purchase price is a rough filter, not a rule. 📌Underwrite conservatively: Add 10% vacancy, 5% expense growth, 50 basis points higher cap rate. 📌Network is your real edge: Off-market deals beat marketed deals every time.
